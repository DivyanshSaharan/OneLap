## Why OneLap?

Work and commuting can fill the stretch from 7 AM to 7 PM. A big adventure is not always realistic. Fifteen minutes outside might be.

OneLap makes that small window easier to use: choose a familiar setting and something to notice, get one brief mission, then leave the app behind.

It is not a route planner, fitness tracker, or conversation to keep open while walking. There is no GPS tracking, compulsory photograph, timer, streak, leaderboard, or feed.

**The goal is to make the screen the shortest part of the experience.**

## Try it

Visit **[onelap-4s17.onrender.com](https://onelap-4s17.onrender.com)** in your phone or desktop browser. No download or installation is required.

1. **Sign in with Google.** Your journal belongs to your verified account.
2. **Make room for a small lap.** Choose 10, 15, or 20 minutes; a courtyard, park, or familiar walking area; daylight or evening; and a focus.
3. **Create your outing.** Review the disclosure before sending these generic selections to hosted Qwen through Tinker.
4. **Head outside.** Read the mission, select **I'm heading out**, and pocket the phone.
5. **Bring back one observation.** Save a note locally and mark the outing completed, stopped, or skipped.
6. **Sync when you choose.** Open **Cloud sync & privacy**, check Atlas status, review consent, and synchronize pending changes.

On another device, sign in with the same account and choose **Load cloud journal**. You do not need to generate a new mission to read your history.

The hosted demo has a small, shared inference allowance. Mission generation becomes unavailable when its approved spending or request limit is reached. Saved observations are not automatically sent to the model.

**Public-demo boundary:** journal sync is enabled; journal-based AI reflection remains disabled in the last verified deployed configuration. A synced note is not automatically analyzed or used by **Create my small outing**.

## One observation, one new question

An observation shared by the builder on October 11, 2026:

> I thought all dtc blue buses are electric. But when I went pass one of them, I heard the sound of diesel engine. And to my surprise it was not an EV.

### Reflection

> An engine-like sound made you question your assumption that every blue bus is electric. The interesting discovery is the contrast between what you expected and what you heard. The sound you described does not, by itself, establish the bus's fuel type.

### Next mission: Describe before deciding

> On your next familiar lap, notice one sound from wherever you already are. Describe its rhythm or tone without deciding what caused it. If nothing stands out, skip the task.

**Bring back:** one description of a sound, and one thing you are still unsure about.

The observation above was supplied by the builder, who reports synchronizing it to Atlas. This reflection and next-mission wording were written for the README, not captured from Qwen or generated by the deployed app. They show the intended experience; they are not a model-test result. This example uses a sounds focus; a generated follow-up must preserve its actual source mission's focus, duration, setting, and conditions.

### How personalization works

When the follow-up feature is enabled, you select one completed, synced observation and optionally up to two earlier completed notes. After reviewing exactly what will be shared, you approve one request.

The server retrieves only those account-owned records from Atlas. Qwen is instructed to:

- Reflect briefly on what you actually reported, without inventing surroundings, causes, feelings, or progress.
- Make a gentler or different activity when feedback says **too difficult** or **not for me**.
- Keep the original duration, setting, conditions, and focus.
- Suggest one phone-free activity rather than repeat the previous instruction.

You review the suggestion and choose **Use this next mission** or discard it. Saving, syncing, and reconnecting do not trigger this flow. Journal notes do not automatically retrain the model.

## Open-weight AI at the core

OneLap uses **[Qwen3.5-4B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B)** through **Thinking Machines' Tinker**. A completed task-specific LoRA pilot trained the model for short, structured missions and feedback-sensitive follow-ups. The approved deployment configuration selects that trained adapter.

Open weights make the model's behaviour adaptable, not just its surrounding prompt. The application keeps its validation, consent, journal ownership, and spending rules separate from the provider.

Today, inference is hosted on Tinker. OneLap does **not** run the model locally, provide free inference, or claim that open weights alone keep shared text private. A different compatible runtime is an architectural option, not an implemented local deployment.

## Fine-tuning: a measured improvement

One rank-16 LoRA candidate was trained through Tinker with **100 synthetic supervised examples**, **three epochs**, and **30 updates**.

Scenario families were separated before their variants were authored. Base and adapter captures used unchanged prompts, checks, and decoding, with one sample per input.

| Measurement                        | Base Qwen     | Fine-tuned adapter |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------- | ------------------ |
| Development application acceptance | 16/20 — 80%   | 20/20 — 100%       |
| Held-out application acceptance    | 20/30 — 66.7% | 29/30 — 96.7%      |
| Held-out JSON/schema validity      | 24/30 — 80%   | 30/30 — 100%       |

**Held-out application acceptance improved by 30 percentage points.** This is structured-output and application compliance, not 96.7% overall accuracy, a safety guarantee, or evidence that the outings are enjoyable.

The pilot is small and synthetic, with correlated variants and independent human semantic review still pending. One adapter reply was rejected because a conservative keyword check matched **"without recording it"**. That failure stays in the result.

The pilot reserved **$0.285662** for training and 100 evaluation samples under a separate $0.75 token allowance. Reservations are estimates, not an invoice; checkpoint storage is excluded.

## Architecture

| Layer             | Technology                | Role                                                                   |
| ----------------- | ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Browser           | React, TypeScript, Vite   | Mission preparation, pocket view, observation journal                  |
| Local persistence | IndexedDB, service worker | Saved missions and notes; cached public app shell                      |
| API               | FastAPI, Pydantic         | Authentication, structured validation, controlled generation           |
| Model             | Qwen3.5-4B, Tinker, LoRA  | Task-specific missions and optional reviewed follow-ups                |
| Cloud data        | MongoDB Atlas             | Account-scoped journals, revocable sessions, durable spending counters |
| Identity          | Google sign-in            | Backend-verified identity and journal ownership                        |
| Deployment        | Docker, Render            | Frontend and API behind one HTTPS origin                               |

**Initial mission:** generic selections → Tinker/Qwen → application validation → local mission.

**Journal:** observation → local save → explicit Atlas sync → account-scoped cloud history.

**Optional follow-up:** selected Atlas notes → separate sharing approval → Tinker/Qwen → validation → review → accept or discard.

Atlas synchronization is storage, not inference. No fake-model fallback replaces an unavailable or rejected response.

## Privacy and offline behaviour

- **No GPS, raw photos, or audio:** initial generation accepts only generic mission selections.
- **Local first:** observations save on the current device before any cloud upload.
- **Explicit sharing:** syncing to Atlas and sending selected notes to Tinker are separate actions.
- **Account isolation:** verified Google identity controls cloud access and separates local journal slots.
- **Offline limits:** a previously verified signed-in tab can read its saved mission and save a note while offline. A fresh reload needs online session verification; new inference also requires connectivity.
- **Not encrypted locally:** signing out hides account history but does not erase browser storage.
- **Deletion is explicit:** removing a note clears its local content and, when applicable, queues cloud deletion for the next approved sync. Atlas keeps a content-free tombstone to prevent replay.
- **No automatic analysis or training:** saving, signing in, loading history, or reconnecting does not upload notes or call Qwen.
- **No navigation or safety assessment:** choose your own familiar area and skip or stop if conditions are unsuitable.

## Run locally

Requires **Python 3.13** and **Node.js 22.12 or newer**. Run these PowerShell commands from the repository root:

```powershell
python -m venv .venv
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m pip install -r backend\requirements-dev.txt
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m pip install -r backend\requirements-journal.txt
npm.cmd ci
```

Copy `.env.example` to an ignored `.env` only if you do not already have one, then configure your private credentials. Hosted inference also requires `backend/requirements-ai.txt`. Spending, journal sharing, reflection sharing, and tracing stay disabled in templates until deliberately configured.

Start the API:

```powershell
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m uvicorn onelap.main:create_app --factory --app-dir backend --host 127.0.0.1 --port 8770 --workers 1 --no-proxy-headers
```

In a second terminal:

```powershell
npm.cmd run dev
```

Open [localhost:5174](http://localhost:5174/). Configure Google's approved browser origins and backend authentication before using sign-in. Never place credentials in browser build-time variables or commit `.env`.
